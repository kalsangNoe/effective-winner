## TL;DR
David Perell interviews author Jimmy Soni (*The Founders*, a forthcoming Kobe Bryant book) about the mechanics of a serious writing practice and where AI actually fits into it. The throughline is that craft is built from obsessive research, protected time, and cross-training across formats — and that AI (specifically Claude) accelerates the "middle" of that process without replacing the human work of vision-setting and fact-checking at the ends. Soni also uses Kobe Bryant's secret writing career as an extended case study in what it looks like to take a craft seriously purely out of love rather than ambition. The conversation closes with Soni's critique of traditional publishing and his own coffee-table book about a hand-restored Brooklyn carousel.

## Key ideas
- **Kobe Bryant as a hidden case study in craft.** Kobe journaled daily for 15 years, studied Joseph Campbell and Hemingway, ghostwrote his own shoe ads, wrote rap to learn compression, wrote and self-produced the poem "Dear Basketball" (which became an Oscar-winning animated short with John Williams and Glenn Keane), and rejected big-publisher book deals to start his own publishing company because they wouldn't invest in the quality he wanted (e.g., spending two weeks re-architecting a book's barcode).
- **Love, not ambition, is the sustaining fuel.** Soni: "Ambition, it's a fuel that burns relatively clean for a while and then it can not burn so clean... the true greats, I think it has to come from love." Kobe sought out other obsessives (George R.R. Martin, Jony Ive, Bruce Lee's writings, Taylor Swift) not for their fame but because they shared his intensity of devotion to craft.
- **The 4am schedule is about protecting energy, not virtue.** Soni writes 4–8/9am daily, seven days a week, before email or admin, because that's when his "premium fuel" is available and nobody can interrupt him. "The world is a conspiracy designed to prevent writing from happening."
- **Research is often disguised procrastination.** Soni's fix: a structured morning — 10–15 minutes reading a "model book" (an on-ramp), then a timed 30-minute research block, then writing with a set word count. For deep projects (like the six-year PayPal book), most research happens blended into writing itself, dug up fact-by-fact as needed, rather than front-loaded.
- **The Ed Bogus story — a parable for research.** While researching *The Founders*, Soni found an obscure name on a PayPal-predecessor cap table, cold-called him, and learned the investor backed Peter Thiel purely because Thiel's ruthlessness at chess convinced him "anything he did, he would make successful." Lesson: the best anecdotes often come from people on the periphery, not the central figures — Soni calls this the "toenail strategy" (after Michael Lewis's observation that Renaissance painting detail hides in the toenails).
- **Cross-training across formats sharpens the core craft.** Op-eds (700 words) taught Soni word economy that fed back into book writing; he argues even Twitter's character limits improved a generation of writers by forcing compression.
- **Where AI actually helps, in Soni's own workflow:**
  - *Research amplification* — collapsing "10 Google searches into one query," e.g., using Claude to verify Jewish burial customs while editing a grief memoir.
  - *An always-on editor* — feedback on flabby paragraphs or wrong words at 4am, when no human editor is available.
  - *Adversarial red-teaming* — having Claude write a 700-word takedown of his own op-eds ("do not hold back") to find blind spots before publishing.
  - *Numeracy support* — using AI to generate accurate analogies/number-crunching for a "word person" who isn't strong with math.
  - *Anxiety antidote* — using Whisper Flow voice dictation to get a bad first draft out fast (including a dreaded personal apology note) before self-consciousness can block him.
- **AI is "middle to middle," not "end to end."** Quoting Balaji-adjacent thinker "Eastern Vasan" (as transcribed): humans set the vision at the start and verify/fact-check at the end; AI can now do much of the middle work. Soni frames AI as the next tool in a lineage — hand, typewriter, word processor, AI — not a replacement for the writer's own words, research, or interviews.
- **The publishing industry critique.** Traditional publishers have quietly stopped doing marketing, cover design, and thorough editing while still taking the lion's share of revenue; authors increasingly pay out of pocket for what publishers used to provide. Soni co-founded Infinite Books with Jim Kwik(?)/"Jim Oanisy" to offer different deal structures (e.g., authors keeping 70% of royalties, no advance), AI-based plagiarism checks before publication, and AB-tested cover art instead of publisher intuition. He argues AI-driven plagiarism screening should end the era of plagiarism scandals entirely.
- **Publishing's two-week hit-or-die window is broken.** *Boys in the Boat* was ignored at launch and only became a bestseller ~8–9 months later via word of mouth among rowers — a story Soni uses to argue publishers abandon books too fast, unlike his own "evergreen" nonfiction titles that keep finding new readers for years.
- **The Jane's Carousel coffee-table book.** A personal side project: Soni's daughter became obsessed with a restored 1922 carousel in Brooklyn Bridge Park; he tracked down Jane, the woman who hand-restored it over 30 years using X-acto knives to preserve original paint layers, and turned her story into a Rizzoli-style coffee-table book with Fedden — a format that forced him to master captions and visual word economy instead of prose.

## Quotes worth keeping
- "The world is a conspiracy designed to prevent writing from happening." — Jimmy Soni
- "Research is an excuse not to write... you're just using your research time as a way of getting out of the anxious feeling that you feel when you have to put pen to paper." — Jimmy Soni
- "Ambition, it's a fuel that burns relatively clean for a while and then it can not burn so clean... but the true greats, I think it has to come from love. It's the only sustaining force there is." — Jimmy Soni
- "AI is not end to end, it's middle to middle." — Jimmy Soni, quoting a line he attributes to "Eastern Vasan"
- "AI is the sharpest knife that an engineer has ever been given... but it's still a knife. I've still got to cook the meal." — an engineer friend, quoted by Soni
- "Could I write my books by hand? Yeah, Robert Caro does... I'm choosing to use this tool. The words are still my own. The research is still my own. The facts are still my own." — Jimmy Soni
- "What I have are fellow obsessives." — Kobe Bryant, quoted by Soni (from a GQ interview)
- "If you want to find the intricacies, look at the toenails." — Michael Lewis, as recounted by Soni

## Open questions / reflection prompts
- Where in my own writing process am I disguising research (or reading, or "prep") as productive work when it's really avoidance of the harder act of drafting?
- Do I have a "model book" I reread obsessively for a current project, the way Soni rereads *The Everything Store* or *The Almanac of Naval Ravikant*? What would it be, and what specifically am I studying it for?
- Which of my drafts would benefit from an adversarial AI pass — having it argue against my own conclusion before I publish?
- Am I protecting a specific, defended block of time for the highest-leverage writing, or am I giving my best hours to email and admin by default?
- What's my equivalent of the "toenail strategy" — the peripheral, overlooked source or question that could unlock a better story than the obvious central one?
