## TL;DR
Kawasaki delivers his well-worn "Art of Innovation" talk as a top-ten (plus a bonus) list, drawing on his time at Apple, as a VC, and as an advisor to Google. Central thread: innovation isn't about incremental improvement on an existing curve (better ice-harvesting, better ice factories) — it's about jumping to the next curve entirely (refrigerators), which requires defining your company by the benefit it provides, not the thing it currently does.

## Structure (top ten + bonus)
1. **Make meaning, not money** — start from wanting to change the world (Apple: democratize computing, Google: democratize information, eBay: democratize commerce); money follows meaning, not the reverse.
2. **Make a mantra, not a mission statement** — 2-3 words explaining *why* you exist (Nike: "Authentic athletic performance"; FedEx: "Peace of mind"), not a 50-word corporate mission statement.
3. **Jump curves** — the ice-harvesting → ice-factory → refrigerator story. Companies fail to make the jump because they define themselves by what they do, not the benefit they provide.
4. **Roll the DICEE** — great products are Deep, Intelligent, Complete, Empowering, Elegant.
5. **Don't worry, be crappy** — ship revolutionary-but-imperfect products on the next curve rather than waiting for perfection (with the caveat: not for biotech).
6. **Let 100 flowers blossom** — let customers redefine your positioning (Macintosh was "saved" by desktop publishing via PageMaker, not the spreadsheet/database/word-processor use case it was designed for).
7. **Polarize people** — great products (TiVo, Harley-Davidson, Macintosh) are loved and hated; don't fear that.
8. **Churn, baby, churn** — ship v1 in denial of the naysayers, then flip to relentless iteration (1.1, 1.2, 2.0...) once shipped.
9. **Niche thyself** — a 2x2 of uniqueness vs. value; aim for unique + valuable (Fandango, Breitling Emergency watch), avoid "not unique + not valuable" (pets.com).
10. **Perfect your pitch** — customize the intro to the audience; 10/20/30 rule (10 slides, 20 minutes, 30-point font).
11. (Bonus) **Don't let the Bozos grind you down** — distinguishes obvious "loser" Bozos from dangerous well-dressed, rich-and-famous Bozos whose success reads as smart but is often just luck.

## Quotes worth keeping
- "If you truly want to make meaning, it's the first step towards innovation."
- "Most companies define themselves in terms of what they do, not the benefits they provide."
- "Don't worry. Be crappy... it's OK to have elements of crappiness to your revolution."
- "Positioning and branding ultimately comes down to what the consumer decides, not to what you decide."
- "Rich and famous parses too smart. But rich and famous parses too lucky, not smart, at least half the time."
- Historical Bozosity: Thomas Watson (IBM) — "a world market for maybe five computers"; Western Union internal memo (1876) on the telephone being "of no value"; Ken Olsen — "no reason anyone would want a computer in their home."

## Open questions / reflection prompts
- Where am I defining a project by what it currently does rather than the benefit it provides — and what's the "next curve" version of it?
- Do I have a 2-3 word mantra for what I'm building, or just a mission-statement-shaped explanation?
- What am I currently withholding from shipping because it's not "complete" yet, when shipping crappy-but-revolutionary would teach me more?
