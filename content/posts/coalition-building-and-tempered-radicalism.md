Building support for a novel or radical idea requires a Goldilocks dose of familiarity — too radical alienates the moderates you need, too tempered fails to convert anyone. The 19th-century women's suffrage movement is the extended case study.

## Key Concepts
- **Purely radical positioning alienates potential allies.** The split between Lucy Stone (moderate, coalition-oriented) and Susan B. Anthony/Elizabeth Cady Stanton (radical, uncompromising) fractured the U.S. suffrage movement for over two decades — costing it winnable votes. Occupy Wall Street's branding (centered on the radical tactic of occupation itself, rather than a broader "99 Percent" framing) is cited as a modern parallel.
- **Wrap the radical ask inside a familiar frame ("tempered radicalism").** Frances Willard sold suffrage to the conservative Woman's Christian Temperance Union not as a justice argument but as "home protection" — a means to fight alcohol abuse, a cause temperance activists already believed in. The reframe is what unlocked the alliance.
- **Novel ideas land better paired with one familiar reference point.** *The Lion King*'s script got greenlit at Disney only after someone described it as "*Hamlet* with lions" — giving executives a single, familiar handle for an otherwise unprecedented pitch.
- **Convert enemies, not frenemies.** Ambivalent relationships (someone who's sometimes supportive, sometimes undermining) are measurably more stressful and unhealthy than openly negative ones. Converted skeptics, once won over, become more persuasive advocates than people who supported you from the start — because they have to work to justify their change of mind, and their conversion is more credible to other skeptics.
- **Insiders and outsiders judge a coalition by different people.** Insiders judge a movement by its most central figures; outsiders judge it by its most extreme member. Stanton's alliance with the scandal-prone Victoria Woodhull damaged the suffrage movement's outside reputation even though Woodhull wasn't central to it internally.

## Details
The through-line: coalition failure is rarely about the underlying cause being wrong — it's about a mismatch between how radical the *positioning* is and how radical the *audience* is willing to go. The skill is holding the radical goal steady while modulating the frame per audience — home protection for conservatives, justice for the already-converted.
