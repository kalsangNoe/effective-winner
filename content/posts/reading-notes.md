## The premise

Deep work is the ability to focus without distraction on a cognitively demanding task. The claim is that this capacity is both increasingly rare and increasingly valuable.

## Practices that stuck

A few ideas survived contact with my actual schedule.

### Time blocking

Give every hour a job in advance. The plan will be wrong, but planning forces you to confront how little uninterrupted time actually exists.

### Shutdown rituals

A deliberate end to the workday tells your brain it can stop chewing on open loops. The ritual matters more than its contents.

## Where I disagree

The book underrates serendipity and the value of a wandering mind. Not every hour should be optimized; some of the best ideas arrive precisely when you stop hunting for them.
